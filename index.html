Abstract

This essay explores how thermodynamic principles are utilised in Formula One power units to enhance vehicle speed. FIA regulations prohibit teams from increasing vehicle performance from just burning more fuel as fuel flow was restricted to 100kg/h. Therefore, engineers must maximise the conversion of chemical energy to useful mechanical work. The first law of thermodynamics presents the engine as a system that converts energy, while the second law of thermodynamics explains why waste heat is unavoidable and encourages heat recovery. This essay examines how high compression ratios, lean combustion, pre-chamber jet ignition, MGU-H and MGU-K energy recovery systems increase thermal efficiency. It considers 2026 regulations, which remove the MGU-H, increase MGU-K power to 350kW and require sustainable fuel usage. These technologies utilise thermodynamic principles to achieve thermal efficiency, resulting in greater acceleration, straight-line speed and faster lap times from the same amount of fuel. (Global.honda, 2015)

Introduction

According to (Devriendt, 2021), Thermodynamics is the study of the movement of heat in macroscopic objects and is imperative when considering how to enhance speed in F1 vehicles. Formula One cars can achieve high speeds due to high thermal efficiency of the power units, meaning a limited quantity of fuel energy gets converted into useful mechanical work whilst limiting waste heat. To be environmentally conscious, a maximum fuel flow was set in 2014, prompting engineers to enhance thermal efficiency. F1 engines achieve thermal efficiency by applying laws of thermodynamics, optimising combustion, recovering waste heat and managing heat transfer. (Menon, 2026)
